What Pollutants to Measure

  • CO2 — Above 1000ppm causes fatigue and poor concentration. Common in bedrooms and offices.
  • PM2.5 — Fine particles from wildfire smoke, traffic, cooking. Linked to respiratory issues.
  • VOCs — From paint, cleaning products, furniture. Long-term exposure is a health concern.
  • Radon — Canada has some of the highest radon levels globally. Basement monitoring is essential.
  • Humidity — 40-60% is ideal. Too low causes dry air; too high causes mould.

Wildfire Smoke in Canada — PM2.5 Monitoring

Canadian wildfire seasons now regularly push outdoor PM2.5 into dangerous territory. A good indoor monitor tells you whether your home filtration is keeping your indoor air safe. Pair with a HEPA air purifier for full protection.

❓ Frequently Asked Questions

What is a good CO2 level for indoor air?
Under 800ppm is excellent. 800-1000ppm is acceptable. Above 1000ppm causes fatigue and concentration issues. Above 2000ppm is poor — open windows or improve ventilation immediately.
Do I need an air quality monitor in Canada?
Canada has some of the world's highest radon levels and increasingly severe wildfire seasons. An air quality monitor with PM2.5 and radon detection is particularly useful for Canadian homes.
